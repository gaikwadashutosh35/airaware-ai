Cut indoor smoke at the source

Incense, mosquito coils, candles and tobacco all add smoke indoors. Using them less, or not indoors, is the simplest indoor-air step.

Why?

Ways to improve indoor air

Cooking and smoke

  • Use the kitchen exhaust while cooking

    Switch the exhaust or chimney on before you start cooking and leave it running for a few minutes after.

    Why?

    Cooking gives off smoke and fine particles, and removing them where they start comes first.

    Some kitchen hoods recirculate air instead of venting it outside.

  • Consider a cleaner cooking fuel where possible

    Where it is practical, cooking with LPG or electricity cuts smoke at the source.

    Why?

    Cooking with wood, dung, crop waste, coal or kerosene is a major cause of household air pollution.

Ventilation

  • Air out while you cook or burn incense

    Open windows or switch on an exhaust fan while you cook or burn incense or candles.

    Why?

    Fresh air carries indoor smoke away, but on polluted days it also brings outdoor particles in.

    There is no single rule for windows: it depends on what is happening indoors and outside.

Dust and cleaning

  • Clean with a damp cloth or mop

    Dust surfaces with a damp cloth and mop floors instead of sweeping them dry.

    Why?

    Dry sweeping and dusting lift settled dust back into the air.

Filtration

  • If you use an air purifier, match it to the room

    A portable purifier works on one room. Choose one rated for that room's size, and keep its doors and windows closed while it runs.

    Why?

    A purifier works best in a closed room that matches its rating.

    A purifier does not remove every pollutant.

Better habits

Small swaps that cut smoke and exhaust around you. We never show an emissions saving we can't calculate.

Daily commuting and vehicle idling

  • Switch off the engine while you wait

    Turn the engine off at red lights and while waiting, instead of idling. For some trips, public transport or sharing a ride may be practical.

    Why?

    An idling engine adds exhaust to the air around you and the people nearby.

    We do not estimate emission savings, because we have no documented method for your vehicle and trips.

Waste

  • Don't burn waste or leaves

    Hand waste and garden leaves to municipal collection or composting instead of burning them; open burning adds smoke for the whole neighbourhood.

    Why?

    India's Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 say households must not burn their waste on streets or in open public spaces.

Cooking

  • Consider a cleaner cooking fuel where possible

    Wood, dung or crop waste fires make a lot of indoor smoke. Where it is practical, cooking with LPG or electricity cuts this smoke at the source.

Energy use

  • Choose star-labelled appliances

    When you buy an appliance, compare its Bureau of Energy Efficiency star label: more stars means it uses less electricity for the same job.

  • Use fans first, and keep the AC at 24 °C

    A ceiling or table fan is the first line against the heat. When you use an AC, keep it at its 24 °C default rather than colder.

  • Switch off lights you are not using

    Turn lights off when you leave a room or no longer need them.
